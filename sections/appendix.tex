\paragraph{Input preprocessing.}
MOSTA inputs use the original \texttt{count} layer and a 30,235-entry gene vocabulary. Existing center tokens are inherited from the prepared source data. Neighborhood profiles average normalized expression over the full gene space before selecting up to 512 tokens and assigning 31 nonzero expression bins, with bin zero reserved for padding. The inherited preprocessing uses library normalization to $10^4$, $\log(1+x)$, and section-specific nonzero gene medians; genes with fewer than ten nonzero observations use a fallback median. Target sections supply their own gene statistics and use the inherited vocabulary. Whole-section readout standardization, PCA, and clustering are fitted on target-section positions; method-specific settings are given in Appendix~\ref{app:dual-view}.

HESTA preprocessing starts from \texttt{layers["counts"]}. Within each section, genes present at fewer than three positions are filtered, counts are normalized to $10^4$ per position and log1p-transformed, then scaled by nonzero gene medians. Genes with fewer than ten nonzero values use the median of the stable gene medians as fallback. The 57,350-entry vocabulary, including padding, is constructed from gene names across the 75 count-bearing sections. Center and neighborhood tokens are built with the same top-512 and expression-bin scheme; neighborhood expression is averaged before token selection. Each held-out section supplies its own preprocessing statistics. Mouse and human models use separate vocabularies and weights.

Relative coordinates are centered at the focal position and scaled by the maximum distance within its 128-neighbor window, then sliced to smaller supports. Granularity counts observations; its physical extent depends on the sampled tissue. Maps preserve the measured lattice and leave missing positions empty, without label interpolation.

\subsection{Regional scale preferences and anatomical cases}
\label{app:region-scale}

\paragraph{Regional readout.}
Table~\ref{tab:regional-spatial-iou-full} uses all annotated regions in each of the eight held-out sections: 43 distinct regions and 137 section--region observations. ZoomST Spatial at granularities 8, 32, and 128 and the six external methods use the frozen representations and full-section feature transforms specified in Appendix~\ref{app:dual-view}. Each ZoomST Spatial representation is standardized per feature dimension and projected separately to 32 principal components. External methods retain their original feature preprocessing, including BANKSY's weighted representation with $\lambda=0.8$ and no second feature standardization. These feature coordinates remain fixed during the regional readout.

For each section and clustering seed, we sample without replacement the same number of positions from every annotated class, using the size of the smallest class. All methods and granularities share these sampled indices and their shuffled order. MiniBatchKMeans fits these balanced positions with the number of clusters set to the section's annotation count, k-means++ initialization, ten initializations, batch size 4,096, at most 300 iterations, and no-improvement limit ten. It then predicts all original positions in the section, including the fitting positions. A global one-to-one Hungarian assignment maximizes cluster--annotation overlap on the full section, and region-wise IoU is computed from all positions. Seeds 42, 43, and 44 repeat the sampling and clustering.

We first average the three seeds within each section--region observation, then report the mean and sample SD across the sections containing that region. A region present in one section has one reported value. The final table row equally averages the 43 region means and reports their sample SD. Rows are ordered by each region's mean neighborhood homogeneity across its available sections.

\begin{samepage}
\paragraph{Neighborhood geometry and scale preference.}
Let $y_i$ be the anatomical label at measured position $i$, and $N_k(i)$ its $k$ nearest other positions by Euclidean distance in the section's spatial coordinates. Neighbor searches use all measured positions in the section. For the positions $R$ belonging to one annotated region, we define
\begin{align}
H_{128}(R) &= \frac{1}{|R|}\sum_{i\in R}\frac{1}{128}\sum_{j\in N_{128}(i)}\mathbf{1}[y_j=y_i],
\label{eq:region-homogeneity}\\
B_8(R) &= \frac{1}{|R|}\sum_{i\in R}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\exists j\in N_8(i):y_j\ne y_i\right].
\label{eq:region-boundary}
\end{align}
\end{samepage}
$H_{128}$ measures local annotation homogeneity; $B_8$ is the fraction of positions touching a different annotated region within their eight-neighbor window. The geometry is computed before any clustering-fit subsampling. For each section--region observation, the coarse-scale gain is $\Delta\mathrm{IoU}=\mathrm{IoU}_{128}-\mathrm{IoU}_{8}$, using the seed-averaged Spatial scores. Figure~\ref{fig:region-geometry} shows all 137 observations and pooled least-squares trend lines. Spearman correlations use average ranks for tied values. We report both the pooled correlations ($0.470$ for $H_{128}$ and $-0.408$ for $B_8$) and the equal-weight mean of the eight within-section correlations ($0.409$ and $-0.337$). Positive homogeneity correlations and negative boundary correlations each occur in seven of eight sections.

\paragraph{Source-section anatomical illustration.}
The Lung and Smooth muscle maps in Figure~\ref{fig:results1-mosta} illustrate how context interacts with regional shape. In their fixed visceral domain, Lung IoU increases from 0.894 at granularity 8 to 0.955 at 128. Smooth muscle's narrow bands and rings are better separated at granularity 8, with IoU 0.779 compared with 0.580 at 128.

The anatomical granularity analysis uses E14.5 E1S1 and the frozen MOSTA model defined in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Its 102,519 measured positions supply neighborhood context; scoring uses the fixed union of Heart (2,952), Lung (2,011), Liver (6,015), GI tract (1,260), Smooth muscle (1,737), and Pancreas (425). Each output is standardized and projected by full-SVD PCA 32 on these 14,400 positions. MiniBatchKMeans uses six clusters, batch size 2,048, three initializations, at most 200 iterations, no-improvement limit 30, and seed 42. Atlas annotations define the domain, cluster count, post-hoc maximum-overlap one-to-one assignment, and scores. They do not fit the feature transform or clustering.

Scores are computed over all positions in the fixed evaluation domain. The maps preserve measured coordinates without smoothing or interpolation. Coarse and fine maps share viewing limits and the same readout settings.

For class $c$, predicted and annotated position sets $P_c,T_c$ within the fixed scoring domain define
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IoU}_c=\frac{|P_c\cap T_c|}{|P_c\cup T_c|}
=\frac{\mathrm{TP}_c}{\mathrm{TP}_c+\mathrm{FP}_c+\mathrm{FN}_c}.
\label{eq:iou}
\end{equation}
TP counts correct assignments, FP extra assignments, and FN missed targets.

Adjusted mutual information (AMI) measures the information shared by cluster and annotation assignments after subtracting chance mutual information and dividing by mean marginal entropy minus that same chance expectation. The adjusted Rand index (ARI) measures chance-corrected agreement in whether pairs of positions share a class. Both are invariant to cluster renaming.
