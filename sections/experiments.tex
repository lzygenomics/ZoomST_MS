\section{Evaluation design}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Pretraining and held-out evaluation.}
ZoomST is pretrained on 45 MOSTA mouse embryo sections~\citep{chen2022mosta}, with eight sections reserved in advance, one from each sampled stage from E9.5 to E16.5. An independent human model uses 68 HESTA sections~\citep{pan2026hesta}, with seven sections held out across Carnegie stages CS12--13 to CS23. Supervision uses only granularities 8, 32, and 128. Held-out representation and transformation tests freeze the encoders and transition; input preprocessing and target-fitted readouts follow Appendices~\ref{app:preprocessing} and~\ref{app:dual-view}. The No Cross comparison is an equal-budget controlled ablation: the final checkpoints have the same total number of training updates within each atlas, and No Cross omits cross-granularity supervision. Held-out tests evaluate generalization, controlled objective ablations assess the training losses, and source-section anatomical cases interpret granularity-dependent representations and responses. Table~\ref{tab:evaluation_protocol} specifies data use for each analysis.

\paragraph{Retained tissue information and correspondence.}
Whole-section clustering compares frozen ZoomST with public pretrained and target-fitted spatial methods on both atlases, sharing positions, annotation-defined cluster counts, and downstream readouts within each benchmark while retaining method-specific preprocessing (Appendix~\ref{app:dual-view}). AMI and physical 32-neighbor retention measure anatomical and local spatial organization. Granularity-specific Joint AMI compares ZoomST and No Cross on both atlases. Stage-balanced Brain observations evaluate developmental ordering by diffusion-pseudotime--stage correlations; a separate linear probe predicts stage with leave-one-section-out fitting and frozen encoders (Appendix~\ref{app:developmental-ordering}). On MOSTA, an additional CS variant isolates the contribution of JointMMD to these readouts and to cross-granularity CKA (Appendix~\ref{app:objective-ablation}).

\paragraph{Transformation and response generalization.}
Direct neighborhood encodings provide reference targets. Trained-pair state prediction uses all held-out positions; joint-association tests include shuffled-pair controls and projections unused in training. Unseen-target prediction uses 1,024 fixed positions per section at granularities 12, 16, 24, 48, 64, and 96. These target encodings do not enter training or prediction. The same held-out locations evaluate response ordering over four doubling intervals (Appendices~\ref{app:granularity-transfer} and~\ref{app:scale-continuum}).

\paragraph{Anatomical interpretation.}
Regional IoU readouts compare three Spatial granularities and six external methods across all 43 regions in the eight held-out sections, using shared annotation-balanced clustering-fit positions and full-section scoring. Spatial annotation neighborhoods relate the coarse-scale IoU gain to local homogeneity and boundary fraction. A Lung and Smooth muscle case illustrates granularity-dependent anatomy; Facial nerve and Brain response analyses compare predicted change with encoded change and annotated neighborhood composition. These anatomical cases use E14.5 E1S1 and E16.5 E1S1 from the training set; the Brain mapping display uses held-out E16.5 E2S13. Physical extents use the exact nested supports and nominal bin50 calibration. Sampling, domains, and response definitions are specified in Appendices~\ref{app:scale-cases} and~\ref{app:response-meaning}.
