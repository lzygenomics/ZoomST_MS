\subsection{Cross-granularity learning enriches coarse developmental information}
\label{sec:developmental-information}

Anatomical identity does not exhaust the information needed to describe a developing tissue. We therefore test developmental ordering within Brain, using the same 589 observations per stage from the eight held-out sections. Diffusion pseudotime is compared with measured stage through stage-median and spot-level Spearman correlations, with a common root in E9.5 (Appendix~\ref{app:developmental-ordering}). Separating the three granularities first identifies where cross-granularity supervision contributes.

At the coarsest granularity, $k=128$, the full model increases stage-median correlation from $-0.143$ to $0.381$ and spot-level correlation from $-0.239$ to $0.354$ relative to matched-update No Cross (Table~\ref{tab:joint-scale-comparison}). The gains are smaller at $k=32$ and are not uniform at $k=8$. Anatomical agreement remains comparable between models at each granularity. Both also retain the same broad trend: finer representations have higher anatomical agreement, whereas coarser representations retain more physical neighbors. Cross-granularity learning therefore enriches developmental-stage ordering in broad-context representations rather than merely changing their anatomical partition.

This benefit also appears when all three granularities are combined in the joint readout (Figure~\ref{fig:developmental-ordering}). Stage-median and spot-level correlations increase from 0.619 and 0.651 for No Cross to 0.857 and 0.816 for ZoomST, the highest values among the evaluated methods. Together, the granularity-resolved ablation and the common multi-granularity readout show that relationship learning improves the developmental information available in coarse representations and in their combined description.
