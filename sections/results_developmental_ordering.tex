\subsection{Cross-granularity learning enriches developmental information}
\label{sec:developmental-information}

Having examined the two views in zero-shot tissue mapping, we next ask what cross-granularity learning adds to the retained representations. We compare ZoomST with No Cross in an equal-budget controlled ablation, where No Cross retains within-granularity learning but omits cross-granularity supervision. We evaluate their frozen Joint features separately at granularities $r \in \{8,32,128\}$ on held-out MOSTA and HESTA sections.

Fine-granularity AMI improves on seven of eight held-out MOSTA sections and all seven HESTA sections (Table~\ref{tab:cross-granularity-ami}a; Table~\ref{tab:cross-granularity-ami-sections}). Beyond anatomical separation, we next ask whether cross-granularity learning also enriches developmental information within the same tissue.

We examine Brain through two complementary tasks: developmental ordering and stage prediction. Diffusion pseudotime assesses agreement with the observed developmental sequence through spot-level Spearman correlation. A linear readout tests whether stage can be predicted from the representations, training on the remaining sections and evaluating an entire held-out section in each fold (Appendix~\ref{app:developmental-ordering}).

The developmental gains are particularly evident in coarse representations (Table~\ref{tab:cross-granularity-development}b). MOSTA shows its largest ordering improvement at the coarsest granularity, where spot-level correlation increases from $-0.239$ to $0.354$. HESTA shows higher spot-level correlations at every granularity. Stage-prediction MAE decreases relative to No Cross at every granularity in both atlases, with the largest reductions at $r=128$. On MOSTA, adding JointMMD to coarse-support supervision (CS) further improves DPT--stage correlation and reduces stage-prediction MAE at all three granularities (Appendix~\ref{app:objective-ablation}).

We next compare the developmental ordering obtained from Joint features concatenated across all three granularities with that of other methods. ZoomST achieves the highest spot-level DPT--stage correlation on both atlases among methods with complete-stage readouts (Figure~\ref{fig:developmental-ordering}). Together, these results support the value of cross-granularity learning for enriching developmental information, particularly in broad-context representations, while retaining anatomical utility.
