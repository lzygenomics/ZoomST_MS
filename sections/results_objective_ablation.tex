\subsection{Cross-granularity supervision improves correspondence among retained representations}
\label{sec:objective-ablation}

Cross-granularity prediction supplies supervision to the encoders as well as training a reusable transformation. We therefore test whether this supervision makes the retained fine and coarse descriptions easier to relate, before applying any transformation. Starting from the same base-pretrained checkpoint, we continue training separately on three sections with equal update budgets: the within-granularity objective alone, with coarse-support correspondence (CS), or with both CS and joint-distribution matching (JointMMD). Each model is evaluated on the section used for its continuation.

Two comparisons reveal how the directly encoded molecular states are organized across granularities. First, do locations with similar fine descriptions also have similar coarse descriptions? We measure overlap between their representation-space neighborhoods. Second, can a fine description retrieve the coarse description of the same tissue location? We measure same-location Recall@32. Both comparisons use the native states without the transition. Here, neighbor overlap relates two representation spaces; the physical-neighbor test in Section~\ref{sec:dual-view-identification} instead relates representations to tissue coordinates.

CS improves both correspondence readouts in all nine section--granularity-pair comparisons, and adding JointMMD further improves each comparison (Table~\ref{tab:objective-increments}). The complete objective increases $8\to128$ same-location retrieval by 6.6--8.3 percentage points over base-only continuation. On these three adapted sections, learning cross-granularity relationships makes local similarity and same-location correspondence more consistent between the retained fine and coarse descriptions. The complete continuation protocol and readout definitions are in Appendix~\ref{app:objective-ablation}.
