\subsection{Frozen representations recover anatomy in held-out sections}
\label{sec:dual-view-identification}

Zero-shot transfer remains a challenge for pretrained models in single-cell biology~\citep{kedzierska2025zeroshot}. We therefore evaluate ZoomST on eight MOSTA sections excluded from pretraining, comparing whole-section clustering of frozen molecular, spatial, and joint features with atlas annotations (Appendix~\ref{app:dual-view}).

Joint ZoomST features achieve higher AMI than the evaluated public pretrained baselines on every held-out section and remain competitive with target-fitted spatial methods (Table~\ref{tab:representation-summary}a; Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-e125}). We next test whether physical neighbors remain close in the learned features, connecting anatomical recognition to local tissue organization. Joint features achieve the highest mean neighbor retention among the evaluated methods, preserving the spatial view's advantage alongside anatomical agreement (Table~\ref{tab:representation-summary}b; Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-retention}).

The held-out Brain case further illustrates how the two views complement each other (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-brain}). Molecular features recover most Brain positions but include neighboring tissue; spatial features make fewer extra assignments but miss part of the Brain. Combining the views reduces this tradeoff and improves Brain delineation.

An independently pretrained human ZoomST model shows similar advantages in anatomical agreement and local neighbor retention across the seven held-out HESTA sections (Table~\ref{tab:hesta-representation-summary}).
