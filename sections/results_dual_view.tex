\subsection{Frozen representations recover anatomy in held-out sections}
\label{sec:dual-view-identification}

Zero-shot transfer remains a challenge for pretrained models in single-cell biology~\citep{kedzierska2025zeroshot}. We therefore first evaluate ZoomST's zero-shot representation transfer to eight MOSTA sections excluded from pretraining. With encoder weights fixed, we compare whole-section clustering of molecular, spatial, and joint features with atlas annotations (Appendix~\ref{app:dual-view}).

Joint ZoomST features achieve higher AMI than the evaluated public pretrained baselines on every held-out section, with an equal-section mean of 0.577, and remain competitive with target-fitted spatial methods (Table~\ref{tab:representation-summary}a).

To connect anatomical recognition to tissue organization, we next test whether locations that are close in physical space remain neighbors in the learned features. Joint features recover 52.7\% of physical 32-nearest neighbors on average, compared with 50.2\% for the molecular view and 52.3\% for the spatial view (Table~\ref{tab:representation-summary}b). The section-wise results show how this local spatial correspondence varies across held-out tissues (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-retention}).

Whole-section maps complement these aggregate measures by showing the spatial correspondence between recovered tissue assignments and atlas-defined compartments in E12.5 E2S1 (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-e125}). The held-out E16.5 E2S13 Brain case further shows how the two views make complementary errors (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-brain}). Molecular features recover most Brain positions but include neighboring tissue; spatial features make fewer extra assignments but miss part of the Brain. Combining the views reduces this tradeoff, increasing Brain IoU from 0.884 for the molecular view and 0.905 for the spatial view to 0.962.
