\subsection{Frozen representations retain anatomical and developmental information}
\label{sec:dual-view-identification}

We first test whether the retained representations distinguish anatomical compartments on new tissue sections. We cluster frozen features on the held-out MOSTA sections and compare the resulting partitions with atlas annotations. The molecular view $e$ summarizes neighborhood expression, whereas the spatial view $g$ also uses relative spatial arrangement. Here, each view combines its three training granularities; joint features combine both views. Comparing these feature sets asks whether the two views retain complementary information for tissue identification.

The held-out E16.5 Brain case makes this complementarity visible. Molecular features recover most annotated Brain positions but also include neighboring non-Brain locations. Spatial features make fewer extra assignments but omit part of the Brain. Combining the views reduces this tradeoff: Brain IoU increases from 0.884 for $e$ and 0.905 for $g$ to 0.962 for joint features (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-brain}). The E12.5 whole-section maps provide a broader view of the tissue partitions recovered by the joint representation and the comparison methods (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-e125}).

We also examine whether positions that are physically close remain neighbors in representation space. This tests local spatial correspondence alongside anatomical identity. Using the same features as for clustering, $g$ recovers more of each position's 32 physical neighbors than $e$ in seven of eight sections, with mean retention of 52.3\% versus 50.2\%. Joint features retain this spatial correspondence while combining the two views (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-retention}).

Table~\ref{tab:representation-summary} brings these two readouts together in a comparison with other methods across all sampled developmental stages. Agreement between clusters and annotated domains, measured by AMI, is higher for joint ZoomST features than for the evaluated public pretrained baselines on every section. Mean AMI is 0.577, and performance remains competitive with methods fitted to each target section. The matched-update No Cross ablation has a similar mean AMI of 0.575, while neighbor retention increases from 0.504 for No Cross to 0.527 for the full model. The full representation thus combines similar anatomical discrimination with higher local spatial correspondence than No Cross.

\paragraph{Frozen representations preserve developmental ordering.}
Anatomical identity alone does not describe how a tissue changes during development. We computed diffusion pseudotime from stage-balanced Brain observations in the held-out E9.5--E16.5 sections to test whether frozen representations retain developmental ordering. ZoomST achieved stage-median and spot-level Spearman correlations of 0.857 and 0.816 with measured stage, compared with 0.619 and 0.651 for the matched-update No Cross ablation (Figure~\ref{fig:developmental-ordering}). The full model thus better preserves developmental ordering in unseen sections while retaining similar anatomical discrimination.

\paragraph{Cross-granularity learning strengthens coarse-scale developmental information.}
We next separate the three context sizes to ask what is gained from learning transformations between them (Table~\ref{tab:joint-scale-comparison}). ZoomST and No Cross retain comparable anatomical agreement at each scale, with mean AMI differences ranging from $-0.001$ to $0.016$. Both models show the same scale preference: finer neighborhoods yield higher agreement with tissue annotations, whereas coarser neighborhoods retain more physical neighbors. For ZoomST, AMI decreases from 0.585 at $k=8$ to 0.538 at $k=128$, while neighbor retention increases from 35.1\% to 62.5\%.

The developmental readout reveals what cross-granularity supervision adds to these spatially coherent coarse representations. At $k=128$, stage-median Spearman correlation increases from $-0.143$ for No Cross to $0.381$ for ZoomST, and spot-level correlation increases from $-0.239$ to $0.354$. The gain is smaller at $k=32$; at $k=8$, stage-median correlations tie and No Cross has the higher spot-level correlation. Thus, learning relations between scales enriches developmental-stage ordering in coarse joint representations while retaining comparable tissue-identification performance.
