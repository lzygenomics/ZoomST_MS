\subsection{Frozen representations recover anatomy in held-out sections}
\label{sec:dual-view-identification}

Zero-shot transfer remains a challenge for pretrained models in single-cell biology~\citep{kedzierska2025zeroshot}. We therefore evaluate ZoomST on eight MOSTA sections excluded from pretraining, comparing whole-section clustering of frozen molecular, spatial, and joint features with atlas annotations (Appendix~\ref{app:dual-view}).

Joint ZoomST features achieve higher AMI than the evaluated public pretrained baselines on every held-out section and remain competitive with target-fitted spatial methods (Table~\ref{tab:representation-summary}a).

We next test whether physical neighbors remain close in the learned features, connecting anatomical recognition to local tissue organization. Joint features retain these neighborhoods more strongly than the evaluated baselines, preserving the spatial view's advantage alongside anatomical agreement (Table~\ref{tab:representation-summary}b). Section-wise results show how this correspondence varies across held-out tissues (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-retention}).

Whole-section maps show how recovered assignments correspond spatially to atlas-defined compartments in E12.5 E2S1 (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-e125}). The held-out E16.5 E2S13 Brain case illustrates complementary errors (Figure~\ref{fig:dual-view-brain}). Molecular features recover most Brain positions but include neighboring tissue; spatial features make fewer extra assignments but miss part of the Brain. Combining the views reduces this tradeoff and improves Brain delineation.

An independently pretrained human ZoomST model shows the same advantages in anatomical agreement and local neighbor retention across held-out HESTA sections (Table~\ref{tab:hesta-representation-summary}).
